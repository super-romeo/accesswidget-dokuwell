How it works

Many visitors need small adjustments to use a website comfortably: larger text, stronger contrast, a more readable font, highlighted links, or less motion on the screen. At the same time, site owners are increasingly expected to make their sites accessible, and many do not know where their pages fall short.

Access Panel gives you two things. First, a panel your visitors can open on any page to adjust the site to their needs. Second, a monthly check of your pages that shows what to fix in the site itself. The panel helps visitors today; the fixes make the site better for everyone, including people using screen readers, which a panel alone cannot fully help.

You add one line of code to your site or install the plugin for your platform. Visitors see a small button; the panel lets them change text size and spacing, switch to high contrast or a readable font, highlight links and headings, enlarge the cursor and stop animations. Their choices are remembered on their device.

Every month the service checks your pages for common issues: images without alternative text, text with low contrast, links and buttons without a name, form fields without labels, missing or skipped headings, and pages without a language set. Each issue is listed with the page, the element and a plain explanation of how to fix it.

The list is meant for you or your developer. As issues are fixed, they disappear from the next check, and the dashboard shows progress over time. You can export the list to share it with an agency or a freelancer.

A template accessibility statement helps you tell visitors what you have done and how they can report a problem they meet on your site.

Plans are priced by the size of your site, by monthly page views, so a small blog and a larger store each pay for what they use.

A typical first month looks like this. You install the panel and run the first check. The list shows the most common issues across your pages, sorted by how many pages they affect. Your developer fixes the issues that repeat on every page first, such as low contrast in the header or a form without labels, and the next check shows the list getting shorter.

A widget does not make a website compliant with any law or standard by itself, and we do not claim it does. Accessibility depends on how your site is built; the monthly check helps you find and fix the issues.

Questions

Will the widget make my site compliant?

No widget can do that alone. The panel helps visitors, and the monthly check helps you fix issues in the site itself.

How do I install it?

With one line of code or a plugin for your platform.

What does the monthly check look for?

Missing alt text, low contrast, unnamed links and buttons, unlabeled form fields, heading structure and page language.

Does the panel slow down my site?

The panel loads after the page, so it does not delay the content.

Can I use it already?

Not yet. The service is in preparation, and we are checking whether site owners need it.
